key_takeaways:
- Word embeddings encode word meanings in a vector space to capture similarity in
  meanings.
- Techniques for generating word embeddings include neural networks and dimensionality
  reduction.
- Research on word embeddings gained momentum with the introduction of word2vec by
  Google in 2013.
- The field has evolved from high-dimensional representations to more refined approaches
  through advancements in technology.
- Word embeddings can enhance the performance of various NLP tasks, including parsing
  and sentiment analysis.
- Static word embeddings do not handle polysemy and homonymy effectively.
- Multi-sense embeddings differentiate multiple meanings of words using various approaches.
- Contextually-meaningful embeddings like ELMo and BERT provide token-level representations.
- Bio-vectors and game design applications demonstrate the versatility of embeddings.
- Software tools exist for training and visualizing various types of word embeddings.
- Word embeddings can contain societal biases present in their training datasets.
- The example of 'man is to computer programmer as woman is to homemaker' illustrates
  gender bias in word embeddings.
- Word embeddings trained on corpuses like Google News reflect existing stereotypes.
- Careful oversight is needed when applying word embeddings to avoid perpetuating
  biases.
- Research indicates that unaltered training data can amplify biases in word embeddings.

Word embedding is a representation of a word in natural language processing that encodes meanings in vector space. It improves performance in NLP tasks through various generation methods like neural networks and dimensionality reduction.
